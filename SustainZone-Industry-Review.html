Enhanced industry pages · Scope 3 review

For most organisations, Scope 3 is the inventory.

Across aviation, healthcare, retail, facilities management and financial services, Scope 3 typically accounts for 75% to more than 99% of the total footprint — and within that, one or two GHG Protocol categories do almost all the work. Knowing exactly where the carbon sits is what turns a headline target into a credible reduction plan.

Industry 01

Aviation

Practical net zero support for aviation — and it starts with knowing which kind of aviation business you are. Aviation doesn't have one carbon profile; it has four. Airlines, airports, manufacturers and MRO providers each carry their emissions in completely different places.

Where your carbon actually sits

Scope 3 share of total footprint, by sub-segment
The boundary you measure against depends entirely on which one you are
The road to 2050 — consensus reduction mix
SAF does roughly half the work; technology and operations do the rest
Airlines · 20–35% Scope 3

IAG, Lufthansa, Delta, easyJet

Jet fuel combustion is Scope 1 and dominates. Scope 3 is smaller but growing, led by the well-to-tank fuel cycle — ~66% of Scope 3 for easyJet, ~49% for IAG. The work is fuel efficiency and sustainable fuel.

Airports · 95–99%+ Scope 3

Heathrow, AENA, Schiphol

Airports own very little of their attributed carbon — ~95% at Heathrow is "in-the-air" aircraft emissions. The job is splitting in-the-air from on-the-ground, then attacking what the airport controls.

Manufacturers · 99%+ Scope 3

Airbus, Boeing, Rolls-Royce

More than 99% is the lifetime fuel burn of delivered products. Airbus's 2023 deliveries ≈ 549 MtCO₂e of lifetime emissions. For an OEM, the product roadmap is the carbon strategy.

MRO · 70–90% Scope 3

Lufthansa Technik, MTU, AAR

Dominated by purchased materials — titanium, nickel super-alloys, composites — at 40–70%, plus fuel WTT and transport. The lever is materials sourcing, recycling and supplier engagement.

The levers that move the number

  • Fleet renewal: new aircraft are 20–30% more efficient per seat-km; Delta's latest averaged 27% better than what they replaced.
  • Operational efficiency: single-engine taxi, weight reduction, riblet films and flight planning each yield 1–3%; airspace modernisation 5–10% by 2050.
  • Airports: electrify ground support (BA >90% electric/HVO at Heathrow); fixed ground power saves ~80–100 kg CO₂ per turnaround.

Why act now

  • Compliance: SAF blend mandates are the policy backbone — UK 2% in 2025 rising to 22% by 2040; EU ReFuelEU on a similar curve.
  • Tender advantage: answer ESG questions with sub-segment data, not generic claims.
  • Honest roadmaps: changes in fuel, fleet, operations and procurement you can actually defend.
⚠ A reality check we build in

Hydrogen flight has slipped from 2035 to the late 2040s and its expected contribution was cut from 20% to ~6%. Non-CO₂ effects (contrails, NOx) roughly double aviation's effective climate forcing but sit outside most targets. We plan around what's real.

Who we work with: Airports · Airlines · Aircraft & engine manufacturers · MRO providers · Aviation suppliers building tender-ready carbon evidence.
Industry 02

Healthcare

Advancing sustainable healthcare — where the carbon hides in the supply chain, not the building. The footprint concentrates in Category 1: medicines, devices and consumables. The NHS also counts patient travel most corporates ignore — and one inhaler line can be half of a pharma company's footprint.

Where your carbon actually sits

NHS footprint breakdown
~83% is Scope 3 · a uniquely broad boundary including patient travel
When one product line dominates
Share of corporate footprint from pressurised inhalers
NHS & systems · ~83% S3

Health systems

Supply chain ~45%, personal/patient travel ~13%, medicines & gases ~20%, equipment ~10%. The NHS is 4–5% of total UK greenhouse gases.

Hospitals · 70–80% S3

Providers

Supply chain + estate energy dominate, but anaesthetic gases (Scope 1) are a top lever. Operating theatres run at 3–6× normal intensity.

Devices · 85–99% S3

Philips, Medtronic, BD

Materials 55–70%; use phase of imaging/dialysis 15–30%. Idle imaging machines waste 40–91% of their energy — overnight switch-off is real.

Pharma · 80–97% S3

GSK, AstraZeneca, Pfizer

Active ingredients, excipients & packaging 63–81%; cold chain 6–14%. GSK's Ventolin = 49% of total footprint.

The four-step method

Map → Collect → Calculate → Improve

Map the organisation (including NHS patient travel) → collect energy, waste, travel & supplier data → calculate with data-quality flags → report hotspots and ESG-ready outputs for procurement questionnaires.

The levers that move the number

  • Pharma redesign: dry-powder inhalers are 10–25× lower carbon; GSK's HFA-152a reformulation cuts ~92% per inhaler. NHS already down 33% since 2019/20.
  • Anaesthetics: retiring desflurane saved the NHS ~41 ktCO₂e & £4m a year; N₂O leak management a further ~90 ktCO₂e.
  • Estate & devices: heat pumps, NHS solar (~200 projects), MRI switch-off cutting energy 25–33%.

Why act now

The NHS supplier roadmap is a hard deadline

From April 2027 all NHS suppliers must publish carbon reduction plans (Scope 1, 2 & 3). From April 2028, product-level footprints. From April 2030, only suppliers showing progress qualify for contracts. Net zero by 2045.

Who we work with: Hospitals & health systems · Pharmaceutical companies · Medical device manufacturers · Healthcare suppliers preparing for NHS net-zero procurement.
Industry 03

Fashion & Retail

Rethinking fashion and retail for a circular economy — where almost everything is Scope 3. For retailers, resellers and distributors, Scope 3 is 90–98%+ of the inventory, dominated by goods purchased for resale. What differs is which upstream impact dominates.

Where your carbon actually sits

Scope 3 share across retail sub-segments

Fashion lifecycle — where the impact lands

Fashion · 95–99% S3

Inditex, H&M, Nike

Purchased goods 60–80% (fabric ~47% for H&M); use phase 10–25%; transport rising as air freight grows.

Furniture · ~98% S3

IKEA, Wayfair

Materials are the headline — ~46% for IKEA, 79% for Wayfair. IKEA's FY30: −50% materials, −80% production.

Grocery · 75–96% S3

Tesco, Walmart, Amazon

Food & agriculture drive Category 1; FLAG emissions 60–70% for grocers triggers a separate SBT pathway.

Electronics · 95%+ S3

Currys, AO, Best Buy

Purchased goods 40–55%, but appliance use phase 35–50% (Lowe's >80%). Energy-efficient product mix is the lever.

Distributors · 95%+ S3

Bunzl, DCC, Diploma

Purchased goods dominate (spend-based) with transport material. Bunzl targets 79% of Cat-1 suppliers on SBTs.

The workhorse lever

Supplier engagement

Get suppliers ≥67% of Scope 3 to set targets. H&M cut coal-boiler suppliers 117→67 in a year.

The levers that move the number

  • Materials & FLAG: recycled polyester, Better Cotton, FSC wood (IKEA 97%), green steel. No-deforestation becomes mandatory under SBTi FLAG.
  • Circularity & take-back: H&M Sellpy, Zara Pre-Owned, IKEA Buy Back — reinforced by UK Packaging EPR and the EU Digital Product Passport.
  • Use phase: lower-temperature wash design; energy-efficient SKUs for electronics.

Why act now

  • Avoid regulatory risk as EPR, CSRD and DPP rules bite.
  • Gain ESG advantage with clearer, product-level evidence.
  • Unlock circular revenue through resale, reuse, repair and recommerce.
⚠ A data-quality note

Most retail inventories rely on spend-based factors carrying ±30–50% uncertainty that shifts with inflation. We replace these with activity-based data for your largest categories so the numbers withstand audit.

Who we work with: Fashion brands, manufacturers & retailers · Furniture & homeware · Grocery & general retail · Consumer electronics · Distributors & wholesalers.
Industry 04

Facilities Management

Smart facilities management — solving the boundary problem before the emissions problem. The carbon an FM provider influences mostly sits on the client's books. Providers draw that line very differently, which is why two FM inventories can look incomparable.

The key methodological choice

Client-property energy can be (a) a Scope 3 line, (b) a separate "managed emissions" disclosure, or (c) the client's responsibility. Mitie uses (b); CBRE & JLL use (a) with an intensity target; ISS uses (a) as use-of-sold-service. The single most important step is to specify this in the contract to avoid double counting — exactly where SustainZone adds value before a tonne is measured.

>95% S3

Servicing client sites as "use of sold service." SBTi-validated: −46.5% S1+2, −27.5% S3 by 2030.

~99% S3

Sodexo UK&I

Client energy 53%, food 34%, commuting 9%, waste 3%. SBTi −55% by FY30.

~98% S3

~99.6% S3

CBRE GWS / JLL

Managed properties >90% of total, per-sq-ft intensity target. JLL net zero by 2040.

Biggest lever

The managed estate

BMS optimisation, LED retrofits, heat-pump heating, on-site solar + battery, refrigerant phase-out.

How SustainZone delivers

  • Boundary & contract design: agree how energy data flows, then shift from input-based to performance-based contracts so incentives align.
  • Energy monitoring: sub-metering, HVAC optimisation, BMS fault detection — typically 10–20% quick wins.
  • Performance analytics: dashboards against ISO 50001, BREEAM In-Use, NABERS UK and GRESB.

Why act now

  • Cost reduction: 20–30% operational savings; 25–40% energy reductions achievable.
  • Asset value: green, efficient buildings command higher rent and lower vacancy.
  • Regulatory readiness: reporting hygiene that avoids double counting at sector level.

Industry 05

Financial Services

Financing the transition — where your footprint is your portfolio, not your office. For a financial institution, operational emissions are a rounding error. The footprint is Scope 3 Category 15 — financed and facilitated emissions — which is on average over 700× larger than the institution's own operations. The strategy is portfolio measurement, engagement and capital allocation.

Where your carbon actually sits

Your office vs your portfolio
Financed emissions (Category 15) dwarf operations — note the log scale
Where portfolio emissions concentrate
A handful of high-emitting sectors drive most of the financed footprint · illustrative
Private Equity · 99%+ S3

KKR, Blackstone, CVC

GPs influence through ownership and control. Measure portfolio-company emissions, attribute by value share, and act over the hold period. Align via SBTi for PE and the iCI (Initiative Climat International).

Asset Managers · 99.9% S3

BlackRock, Vanguard, Amundi

Financed emissions span listed equity and debt; coverage and data quality are the battle. The lever is stewardship and proxy voting. Disclose via WACI; classify under SFDR Article 8/9.

Banks · 99.8% S3

JPMorgan, HSBC, Barclays

Financed emissions from loans, plus facilitated emissions from capital-markets underwriting. Net-Zero Banking Alliance sector pathways cover power, oil & gas, steel, cement and autos.

Investors · 99.9% S3

Pension funds, insurers, sovereign funds

Asset owners set the expectations that cascade down the chain. Long horizons favour engagement over divestment. Aligned through the Net-Zero Asset Owner Alliance.

The governing alphabet

Frameworks

PCAF for measurement · SBTi FI for targets · NZBA / NZAM / NZAOA for commitment · TCFD / ISSB for disclosure · SFDR for EU funds.

The workhorse lever

Stewardship & engagement

A small set of high-emitting holdings drives most of the portfolio footprint. Engaging them — not just screening them out — is what changes real-world outcomes.

The levers that move the number

  • Engagement & voting: set net-zero expectations on the top emitters that dominate the financed footprint, and escalate through stewardship.
  • Capital reallocation: scale transition and climate-solutions finance; restrict financing of new fossil expansion.
  • Data quality: move the portfolio from estimated PCAF score 4–5 to reported 1–2, and expand coverage across asset classes.

Why act now

  • Disclosure is converging & mandatory: TCFD-aligned reporting, ISSB / IFRS S2, CSRD for large institutions, SFDR for funds.
  • Anti-greenwashing scrutiny (FCA SDR, EU rules) means claims need PCAF-grade evidence.
  • Financed emissions are the risk: they are both the disclosure focus and the real climate-transition exposure.
⚠ Divestment cuts your number, not the molecules

Selling a high-emitting asset to a less-scrupulous owner lowers your inventory while real-world emissions continue. Engagement and transition finance are what actually move outcomes — and they are far harder to fake.

A data-quality note

Most financed-emissions inventories start at PCAF data-quality score 4–5 (estimated/proxy). The credibility step is reported, asset-level data for your largest holdings — exactly the evidence regulators and assurers now expect.

Who we work with: Private equity & GPs · Asset managers · Banks & lenders · Asset owners & institutional investors · Financial institutions building PCAF-aligned financed-emissions inventories.
Closing

The same pattern, every sector

Emissions concentrate somewhere very specific — the use phase for aviation OEMs and electronics retailers, purchased goods and services for healthcare, retail and FM, and financed emissions for financial services. Once you find that hotspot, the levers are well understood. The hard part is governance: the right boundary, a recalculation policy, data-quality scoring and supplier engagement so the numbers survive audit, CSRD assurance and review from CDP, SBTi and PCAF. That's the practical, no-fluff work SustainZone exists to do.
